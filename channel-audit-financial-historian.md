### What is working for them

1. **Ruthless cadence consistency.** Three long-form videos per week, every week, for a year. Growth compounds from volume plus a repeatable format, not from any single viral fluke.
2. **They abandoned Shorts.** 146 Shorts in year one, zero in the last 30 days. Shorts brought ~800K views but long-form brought 7.6M — they read the data and cut the format that wasn't building the channel.
3. **Curiosity-gap question titles.** Their #1 video is "If Every Country Is in Debt… Who's the Creditor?" (1.27M views). Others: "What Survives When Money Fails" (346K), "The Financial Tricks Governments Use to Keep You Broke" (259K). The title poses a question the viewer suddenly realizes they can't answer.
4. **Specific named entities outperform generic topics.** "Al-Ghazali: The Thinker Who Linked Money to Morality" (693K), "The Financial Genius of Sun Tzu" (169K), "Ibn Khaldun" (92K). Specificity signals depth; generic titles ("Wealth vs. Value") sit at ~30K.
5. **A tight 9–13 minute band.** Long enough for mid-roll ads and real depth, short enough to finish. This matches BData's existing 8–11 minute default almost exactly.
6. **One consistent persona and voice.** Faceless but not anonymous — a recognizable narrator identity, consistent branding, "this is the ONLY official channel" positioning.
7. **Description as positioning statement.** Their About text sells a worldview ("context and depth, not just headlines"), states who the channel is for, and ends with a subscribe CTA.

### What NOT to copy

- **Their goal is ad revenue at scale; BData's is 10–15 client relationships.** They need millions of casual viewers; BData needs a few thousand of the *right* nonprofit decision-makers. Do not chase their topic breadth or their view counts.
- **3 videos/week is not sustainable** alongside a full-time job and a PhD, and isn't necessary for a trust-building B2B channel. One per week, kept without fail, is the equivalent discipline at BData's scale.
- **Clickbait tension.** Their titles flirt with outrage ("Keep You Broke"). BData's buyers are program directors and EDs — curiosity works, manufactured outrage undermines credibility.

## Part 3 — The BData channel playbook (imitating the mechanics, not the niche)

**Positioning line for the channel description**, modeled on their About-page structure:

> Welcome to BData Solutions, where data meets the sectors that get overlooked. I help nonprofits, community organizations, and public programs turn messy spreadsheets into evidence funders trust. No jargon, no 40-column trackers — just clear frameworks for proving your program works. New video every week. If you run a program and dread reporting season, subscribe.

**Format formula** (their model, adapted):
- 1 long-form video per week, 9–12 minutes, same publish day/time every week (they publish ~15:00–16:00 UTC consistently — pick one slot and keep it)
- Faceless with the animated avatar as the persona; screen-share/whiteboard visuals as the main canvas
- No Shorts for now — their own data shows long-form built the channel; Shorts are a later experiment, not a launch requirement

**Title patterns to adopt** (mapped to the existing 10-video content bank):
- *Curiosity question:* "If Your Program Works… Why Can't You Prove It?"
- *Contrarian how-to* (the uploaded audio's video): "Stop Tracking 20 Metrics: The KPI Framework That Actually Works"
- *Named framework/entity:* "Results-Based Accountability: The Framework Funders Already Trust"
- *"Nobody teaches you":* "The Reporting Skills Nobody Teaches Nonprofit Staff"

**Thumbnail system:** avatar face + 3–5 word text + one visual motif, brand palette only. Consistency beats cleverness at small scale.

**Success metric:** not views — inquiries. Watch for description-link clicks to the Free Starter Tracker (email capture) and Data Health Check. A 500-view video that produces one $3K dashboard client beats a 50K-view video that produces none.
